## Overview

### Federal Framework

- **SCRA (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)**: Federal law protecting service members' residency rights and preventing states from taxing military income in non-residence states
- **MSRRA (Military Spouse Residency Relief Act)**: Extends SCRA protections to military spouses, allowing them to maintain residency in service member's home state
- **Combat Pay Exclusion**: Federal exclusion for combat zone pay; most states follow federal treatment
- **State Variations**: States have wide latitude in taxing military retirement pay, ranging from full exemption to full taxation

### 2025 Tax Year Key Changes

- **California**: New partial exemption of up to $20,000 (income limits apply)
- **Vermont**: Expanded to full exemption for AGI under $125,000
- **Georgia**: Increased exemption amounts; moving to $65,000 for all ages in 2026
- **Indiana**: Extended exemptions to Space Force, USPHS, and NOAA Corps members
- **New Hampshire**: Eliminated tax on dividend/interest income, effectively becoming no-income-tax state

## SCRA and MSRRA Implementation

### Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA)

**All states must honor SCRA** per federal law. Key protections:

1. **Income Tax Protection**: Service members are not subject to income tax in their duty station state if they maintain legal residence elsewhere
2. **Personal Property**: Vehicles and other personal property not taxed outside home state
3. **Spouse Protection**: Military spouses may claim same state of residence as service member
4. **Tax Bracket Protection**: States cannot increase non-military spouse's tax bracket by including service member's military income

### Military Spouse Residency Relief Act (MSRRA)

**All states must honor MSRRA** per federal law. Key provisions:

1. **Income Tax**: Spouse's income not taxed in duty station state if spouse maintains same residency as service member
2. **Domicile Retention**: Spouse can maintain domicile in service member's home state
3. **Requirements**:
   - Spouse must be in duty station state solely to be with service member
   - Service member must be there under military orders
   - Both must claim same state of domicile

**State Implementation Notes**:
- Most states have updated withholding forms to accommodate MSRRA
- Some states require annual MSRRA certification
- Enforcement mechanisms vary by state

## Combat Pay and Special Military Income

### Combat Zone Pay

**Federal Treatment**: Enlisted personnel and warrant officers can exclude all combat zone pay. Commissioned officers limited to highest enlisted pay rate plus imminent danger/hostile fire pay.

**State Treatment**: Nearly all states follow federal combat pay exclusion. States with specific provisions:

- **California**: 100% combat pay exclusion from state income
- **Minnesota**: Additional tax credits available for combat zone service
- **All other states**: Generally follow federal exclusion

### National Guard and Reserve Pay

| State | National Guard / Reserve Drill Pay Treatment |
|-------|---------------------------------------------|
| Hawaii | Up to $8,636 exempt (2025) |
| Montana | First $15,000 exempt |
| Oregon | Oregon National Guard pay + up to $6,000 other military pay may qualify for subtraction |
| South Carolina | Withholding optional for inactive duty training |
| Connecticut | National Guard and Reserve pay exempt |
| Missouri | Reserve/Guard pay exempt |
| Most others | Generally taxable as regular income unless covered by broader military income exemptions |

### Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) Taxation

**Federal Treatment**: SBP payments are taxable as ordinary income at federal level.

**State Treatment by Category**:

1. **Full Exemption** (46 states): Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming (no income tax), plus Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Connecticut, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, West Virginia, Wisconsin

2. **Partial Exemption** (2 states):
   - **California**: Same $20,000 exemption as military retirement (AGI limits apply)
   - **Vermont**: Same AGI-based exemption as military retirement

3. **States Following Retirement Exemption**: Colorado, Delaware, Georgia, Idaho, Kentucky, Maryland, Montana, New Mexico, Oregon, Utah, Virginia (SBP gets same treatment as military retirement)

4. **Limited/No Exemption** (1 jurisdiction):
   - **District of Columbia**: Same limited treatment as military retirement

## Disabled Veterans - Special Provisions

### Property Tax Exemptions

Many states provide property tax exemptions for disabled veterans, separate from income tax treatment:

| State | Disabled Veteran Property Tax Provision |
|-------|----------------------------------------|
| Colorado | 100% disabled or TDIU status: property tax exemption (expanded 2025) |
| Florida | Extensive property tax exemptions based on disability rating |
| Texas | Property tax exemptions for disabled veterans |
| West Virginia | $20,000 exemption for 100% P&T disabled; tax credit for 90-100% disabled (2024+) |
| Alaska | Property tax exemptions available |

### VA Disability Compensation

**All states**: VA disability compensation is federally tax-exempt and not included in state taxable income.

### Medical Retirement

**Idaho**: Military retirement from medical retirement is fully exempt (regardless of age)

**North Carolina**: Medical retirement qualifies for full exemption (even if under 20 years service)

## Implementation Notes for DTRules

### Data Requirements

To properly implement military tax provisions, the tax return entity needs:

1. **Income Classification Fields**:
   - `military_retirement_income` (separate from other retirement)
   - `military_active_duty_pay`
   - `military_drill_pay` (National Guard/Reserve)
   - `sbp_payments` (Survivor Benefit Plan)
   - `combat_zone_pay`
   - `va_disability_compensation`

2. **Status Fields**:
   - `is_active_duty_military` (boolean)
   - `military_home_state` (if different from filing state)
   - `spouse_military_home_state` (for MSRRA)
   - `is_veteran` (boolean)
   - `veteran_disabled` (boolean)
   - `veteran_disability_rating` (percentage)
   - `age` (for age-based exemptions)

3. **Service Information**:
   - `military_service_years` (for NC 20-year requirement)
   - `medical_retirement` (boolean, for NC and ID)
   - `service_before_1991_10_01` (for Oregon prorated calculation)
   - `became_resident_date` (for Montana 5-year limit)

### State-Specific Calculation Patterns

**No Income Tax States (9)**:
```
IF state IN (AK, FL, NV, NH, SD, TN, TX, WA, WY)
  military_retirement_taxable_state = 0
```

**Full Exemption States (28)**:
```
IF state IN (AL, AZ, AR, CT, HI, IL, IN, IA, KS, LA, ME, MA, MI, MN, MS, MO, NE, NJ, NY, NC, ND, OH, OK, PA, RI, SC, WV, WI)
  military_retirement_taxable_state = 0
  sbp_taxable_state = 0
```

**Age-Based Partial Exemption (CO, GA, MD)**:
```
Colorado:
  IF age < 55: exemption = MIN(15000, military_retirement_income)
  IF age >= 55 AND age < 65: exemption = MIN(20000, military_retirement_income)
  IF age >= 65: exemption = MIN(24000, military_retirement_income)
  military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income - exemption

Georgia (2025):
  IF age < 62:
    base_exemption = 17500
    IF earned_income >= 17500: additional_exemption = 17500
    total_exemption = MIN(base_exemption + additional_exemption, military_retirement_income)
  IF age >= 62 AND age < 65: exemption = MIN(35000, military_retirement_income)
  IF age >= 65: exemption = MIN(65000, military_retirement_income)
  military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income - exemption

Maryland:
  IF age < 55: exemption = MIN(12500, military_retirement_income)
  IF age >= 55: exemption = MIN(20000, military_retirement_income)
  military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income - exemption
```

**Income-Limited Exemption (CA, VT)**:
```
California:
  IF filing_status = single AND agi <= 125000:
    exemption = MIN(20000, military_retirement_income)
  IF filing_status = joint AND agi <= 250000:
    exemption = MIN(20000, military_retirement_income)
  ELSE: exemption = 0
  military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income - exemption

Vermont:
  IF agi <= 125000:
    exemption = military_retirement_income  // Full exemption
  ELSE IF agi > 125000 AND agi < 175000:
    exemption = military_retirement_income * ((175000 - agi) / 50000)  // Partial
  ELSE: exemption = 0
  military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income - exemption
```

**Conditional Exemption (ID, OR)**:
```
Idaho:
  IF veteran_disabled OR age >= 62:
    military_retirement_taxable_state = 0
  ELSE:
    military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income

Oregon:
  IF service_before_1991_10_01 AND service_after_1991_10_01:
    // Prorated based on service periods
    pre_1991_portion = (service_years_before_1991 / total_service_years) * military_retirement_income
    post_1991_portion = military_retirement_income - pre_1991_portion
    military_retirement_taxable_state = post_1991_portion
  ELSE IF service_before_1991_10_01:
    military_retirement_taxable_state = 0
  ELSE:
    military_retirement_taxable_state = military_retirement_income
```

### SCRA/MSRRA Implementation

// SCRA - Service Member Protection
IF is_active_duty_military AND military_home_state != filing_state:

## Testing Scenarios

When implementing state military tax provisions, create test cases for:

1. **Active Duty SCRA**:
   - Service member stationed in State A, legal residence in State B
   - Expected: Military pay not taxed by State A

2. **Military Spouse MSRRA**:
   - Spouse earning civilian income in State A, domiciled in State B (same as service member)
   - Expected: Spouse income not taxed by State A

3. **Age-Based Exemptions**:
   - Retiree age 54 in Colorado with $25,000 military retirement (expect $15,000 exempt)
   - Retiree age 66 in Colorado with $25,000 military retirement (expect $24,000 exempt)

4. **Income-Limited Exemptions**:
   - California single filer, AGI $100,000, military retirement $30,000 (expect $20,000 exempt)
   - California single filer, AGI $150,000, military retirement $30,000 (expect $0 exempt)

5. **Special Cases**:
   - Oregon retiree with 10 years pre-1991 service, 10 years post-1991 service (expect 50% taxable)
   - Idaho disabled veteran under age 62 (expect full exemption)
   - Montana new resident in year 3 of residency (expect 50% deduction)

6. **SBP Taxation**:
   - Surviving spouse receiving SBP in various states
   - Verify same treatment as military retirement in each state
